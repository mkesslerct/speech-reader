Good morning everyone,
It is a pleasure for the Universidad Politécnica de Cartagena to welcome you to the European Workshop on Open Quantum Systems.
I am not a quantum physicist. But when the organisers explained to me what this workshop is about, I found it fascinating.
We are living in a time of major challenges — energy, technology, resources, the digital transformation of almost everything. I am not saying our challenges are greater than those of previous generations. Every generation has had its own. But it is clear that we will not navigate these changes without science.
And when I say science, I do not only mean applied science, the kind that delivers a product next year. I also mean — perhaps especially — fundamental science. The kind you do.
We hear a lot these days about quantum technologies and the promises they hold. What we hear less about is the hard part: real quantum systems do not live in isolation. They exist in the real world, surrounded by noise and environmental interference — and this makes progress enormously difficult. Your field lives precisely at that interface, trying to understand what actually happens there. The quantum technologies of the future — and everything society hopes to gain from them — will be built on that understanding. That is why work like yours, however theoretical it may seem, is part of how we address the challenges of our time.
There is something else I like about this workshop. I am told it began years ago as a small, informal gathering in the United Kingdom — one day, a few groups, a friendly space where doctoral students and young postdocs could present their work among colleagues. That spirit has now grown into a European meeting, but the idea remains the same: giving early-career researchers a place to share their science, to be heard, and to build the collaborations that will shape the field for decades. As a university, there is little we value more than that.
Closing

So thank you to the organisers for bringing this community to Cartagena, and thank you all for coming. I hope you have three productive days of good science and good conversation — and that you also find a little time to enjoy our city.
